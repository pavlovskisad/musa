## Gold carry in traditional markets

Gold's own risk-free rate is about zero, so TradFi carry cannot fund the current tiers. Any gold-denominated return above roughly 1%/yr is a risk premium (credit, short volatility, venue), not carry.

Market facts (no forecast): gold rose about 65% in 2025, hit a spot record of $5,594.82 on 2026-01-29, then fell 9.5% the next day. It closed 2026-11-20 near $3,520-3,540, about 37% below the record and 18.5% down year to date. The Fed hiked to 3.75-4.00% on 2026-09-16 and a December hike is 96-99% priced. Realized vol was 20.9% in 2025 and 29.6% in 2026; GVZ is 34.8.

Rates: SOFR 3.91%, Term SOFR 4.20% (3M) and 4.60% (12M), T-bills 4.23% (13W) and 4.50% (52W).

Contango: COMEX settlements on 2026-11-19 imply financing of 4.7% (2 months), 5.0% (6 months) and 5.1-5.2% (12 months). That is 0.5-0.7 points above Term SOFR, so the implied lease rate is about -0.5%. No public lease benchmark exists; normal observed levels are 0-0.5%, with a spike to 5.7% in February 2025.

Hedging cost for a treasury holding user USD:
- **Long CME futures:** about 5.1%/yr embedded financing plus under 0.1%/yr in fees and rolls.
- **Net of T-bill income:** minus 0.4-0.9%/yr, so worse than simply holding the gold token at 0%.
- **Break-even:** the synthetic route only pays if the USD side earns more than about 5.2%. With 6-7% DeFi stable yields the net is about +1%/yr in gold terms.
- **Perps:** hedging long via Hyperliquid costs 7.7-8.4%, about 2.6-3.2 points more than CME.
- **Operations:** $1M is 28 MGC or 283 1OZ contracts. CME minimum margin was 5% of notional at last verified date, and peaked at 9% in February 2026. 1OZ trades 24/7 since 2026-07-24.
- **Liquidity:** the worst day was -10.8% (2026-01-30), so about 20-25% of hedged notional must sit in instantly liquid cash or T-bills.

Covered calls: live GLD quotes (28 days) pay 1.47% for a 5% OTM call and 0.57% for 10% OTM. My backtest (GLD plus GVZ, Black-Scholes) in gold terms:

| Strike | 2025 | 2026 YTD | 2010-2024 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5% OTM | -12.6%/yr (breached 48% of windows) | +9.0% | +0.3% to +2.5% |
| 10% OTM | -2.2% | +5.4% | about +0.9% |

The worst 30-day window gave up 15.5% of the ounces. GLDI's record corroborates this: +29% against GLD's +44% over one year.

What this means for the product: the tiers need 8.5-16.5%/yr in gold terms, and carry delivers 0-2%. Using the task's own ratios (about 0.29 / 0.56 / 1.14 points of bonus per 1% of yield), a 1.5-3%/yr sustainable yield supports bonuses of roughly 0.5-1% / 1-2% / 2-3.5% for 6/12/24 months. Anything above that must be labelled as risk-premium harvesting or as subsidy from a marketing partner or miner discount.

Not verified live: CME's site blocked automated access, so margin and contract details come from press reports and search snippets; the current margin after the 2026-11-20 selloff is unknown.

## Gold tokens and yield-bearing gold

SETTLEMENT. PAXG is the right settlement asset for a Base product. Paxos now issues PAXG natively on Base (announced 2026-11-06; token 0xe904...98Ef, its own SupplyControl contract, LayerZero OFT route to Ethereum), so the "Wormhole vs LayerZero" question in ARCHITECTURE.md is closed. Base is thin though: 768 PAXG (~$2.7M) on-chain, ~$1.75M of DEX depth, a $0.6M Morpho market. Ethereum has ~$32M DEX depth and ~$108M/day CEX volume, and Paxos primary creation is reported as free (redemption 0.125% on the first $2M per 30 days). Source through Paxos primary or Ethereum and bridge; use Base pools only for small tickets. XAUT/XAUt0 has no Base deployment (15 networks, Base absent), charges 0.25% each way with a 50 XAUT minimum, and is El Salvador-regulated with quarterly attestation: keep it as a backup behind a token interface, held on Ethereum. KAU, XAUm, PGOLD, DGLD, CGO and VNXAU are too small, too illiquid or not on Base (DGLD is on Base with ~$6K of 2% depth and a reported bridge-mint exploit in Feb 2026).

YIELD. Holding or lending plain gold tokens earns 0% (Aave v3: $51M XAUT supplied, $0 borrowed), so the doc's "Aave PAXG 1-3%" assumption is wrong. Yield-bearing gold tokens exist and have actually delivered about 2-3.7% a year in gold terms: PAXGy 2.6-3.1% (gold leasing, $5.7M, 75% on lease), GLDY 3.2-3.7% (leasing via Monetary Metals, ~$13M, on Base), XAUE 2.2% (opaque quant plus lending, ~$64M), thGOLD ~2-2.3% (loans to a Singapore jeweller, $12.6M), XGLD ~3% (crypto funding carry, $2M), yoGOLD ~0.4%. PAXG-XAUT LP earns 0.5-2.3%. All are small and all pass losses to the holder. Each has an access catch: PAXGy excludes US and EU and lives on Ethereum/X Layer; GLDY is accredited-only, whitelisted, quarterly redemption with an exit fee; XAUE and thGOLD mint only for whitelisted institutions.

WHAT IT MEANS. At a 3% gold-denominated yield on 100% of the reserve the treasury covers roughly 35% / 24% / 18% of the required return on the 6/12/24-month tiers, i.e. self-funded headline bonuses near +0.9% / +1.7% / +3.4%, and realistically half of that once a liquid PAXG sleeve is kept for linear deliveries. Yield-bearing gold is a floor under the bonus, not the engine. The gap to 8.5-16.5% has to come from USD-side carry (scenario D), partner subsidy, or lower bonuses. For scenario B, 2-4% a year in gold is the market price of borrowed gold, so that is the fair benchmark for a partner's reserve.

## Gold as loan collateral

Borrowing stablecoins against tokenised gold is a funding leg, not a yield source. At a safe 30-40% LTV it adds roughly +0.3% to +1.4% per year in gold terms, against the 8.5% / 12.5% / 16.5% the tiers require.

**Where gold can be pledged (live 2026-11-21).** About $80-85M of gold tokens sit in on-chain lending, under 2% of the $4.43B PAXG+XAUT market cap.
- **Aave v3 Ethereum:** XAUt only, e-mode LTV 70% / liquidation threshold 75% / penalty 6%. Cap headroom is 5,525 XAUt (~$19.7M). Borrow costs 3.6% USDC, 4.1% USDT, and rates would not move.
- **Morpho Base PAXG/USDC:** LLTV 77%, created 2026-10-27, used by Coinbase's gold loans. Borrow is 5.7%; only $24K is idle, but Steakhouse vault caps total $25M.
- **Morpho Ethereum XAUt/USDT:** 4.7% now, 4.8% mean over 22 months, spikes to 29%.
- **Others:** Fluid (threshold 80%, penalty 3%, USDT 6.7%, USDC 14.6%), Aave v4 PAXG (75%, cap 2,500 PAXG), Kamino (75%, USDG 5.1%).
- Supplying gold earns 0% everywhere.

**Net carry = LTV x (deployment yield - borrow cost).** At 40% LTV and 5% borrow: -0.4% / +0.4% / +1.2% / +2.8% for yields of 4 / 6 / 8 / 12%. To fund the 12-month tier alone, the spread would need to be 31% at 40% LTV.

**Stress test on the real path** (peak close $5,496 on 2026-01-29 to $3,501 low; -38% intraday; worst 24h -13.4%, 7d -21%). For a position opened at the peak and left alone, with 5% interest accruing:

| Entry LTV | Max LTV reached | Outcome at Morpho 77% |
|---|---|---|
| 25% | 40.9% | safe, health factor 1.90 |
| 40% | 65.4% | safe, health factor 1.19 |
| 50% | 81.7% | liquidated 2026-11-19 (Aave 75%: 11-18) |
| 60% | 98.1% | liquidated 2026-03-23 |

Across every entry day in the year, 0% of entries at 45% LTV or below were liquidated, 2% at 50%, and 52% at 60%. A managed 40% book (repay at 45%) needed three deleveragings totalling 29% of the debt.

**Incidents.** Morpho gold markets show about $325K of liquidations in 2025-26 and zero bad debt. The 2026-03-23 cluster matches RedStone's account of Aave's largest XAUT liquidation day, handled without disruption. The one loss on record is a misconfigured Morpho PAXG oracle in October 2024.

**Oracle.** Aave, Morpho and Fluid price off Chainlink XAU/USD, which closes Friday 17:00 ET to Sunday 18:00 ET. Read live during this close, the feed alternated between $3,575.31 and about $3,532-3,538 every 3-5 minutes on both Ethereum and Base.

**Gold-on-gold.** PAXG-XAUT LP pays 0.5-2.3% on about $11M total TVL; the whole fee pool is $134-170K per year. Looping it on Fluid is negative carry.

**Recommended policy.** Target 30% LTV, hard cap 40%, repay at 45%, emergency at 50%. Pledge at most half the reserve, keep at least 35% of borrowed stables instantly redeemable, and use at most 25% LTV for anything illiquid.

## Dollar stable yield

USD stable yield is a float-management tool, not a bonus engine. Over the 12 months to 2026-11-20 the 3-month T-bill averaged 3.84% (4.34% now, after a Sept 2026 Fed hike). Against that, realized 12-month averages were: tokenized T-bill funds about 3.5% net; sUSDS 3.78%; Aave v3 USDC 3.45% on Ethereum (below T-bills on 86% of days) and 3.70% on Base; Morpho prime USDC vaults on Base 4.0-4.6%; sUSDe 4.30%; syrupUSDC 5.09%; JAAA about 4.5%; surviving Tier-3 dollars 5.5-8%; HLP about 7.5% (flat for six months).

Daily-rebalanced policy simulations realized: conservative (Tier 0-1) 3.7-3.9%, i.e. -0.1 to +0.1pp versus T-bills; moderate (30% Tier 2) 4.2% (+0.3pp); aggressive (40% Tier 2, 20% Tier-3 survivors) 4.8% (+1.0pp). Current run-rates are 4.2-4.5% / 4.8% / 5.5% because rates rose. DeFi risk was therefore paid roughly 0pp in Tier 1, +0.5-1.3pp in Tier 2 and +2-4pp in Tier 3 before losses. One 5% position in a blow-up erases about five years of the aggressive premium.

The incident record is dense. USDe printed $0.65 on Binance (Oct 2025). Stream xUSD fell 77% with about $285M of contagion and Elixir deUSD was wound down (Nov 2025). Resolv USR fell about 75%, leaving Fluid with over $10M bad debt and about 15 Morpho vaults hit (Mar 2026). The $293M Kelp exploit left Aave with $124-230M of potential bad debt, $12B of outflows and USDC pinned at 100% utilization for 4-5 days (Apr 2026). apxUSD depegged in June and is at $0.93 today. Moonwell on Base had repeated oracle incidents including $8.7M (Aug 2026), with suppliers still stuck. A $6.3B Binance incident (Oct 29, 2026) doubled cbBTC/USDC borrow rates and stalled Maple's queue for 25-48h. On Nov 19-20, 2026 USDS supply fell about 40% in a day (redemptions were processed). Steakhouse's prime vaults and Maple's syrup pools reported zero bad debt throughout; losses concentrated in long-tail collateral and high-yield curator vaults.

On Base the usable venues are narrow: Morpho USDC vaults (about $1.9B, 93-100% in one cbBTC/USDC market at about 90% utilization), Aave v3 USDC ($180M supply), sUSDS via the Spark PSM, deJAAA ($23M) and whitelisted T-bill tokens (BENJI, WTGXX, USTBL, mTBILL, JTRSY). Compound is $8M and Pendle has no stable PT depth.

For the product: the liability is grams and the required return is 8.5-16.5%/yr in gold terms. Stable yield covers 3.7-4.8% in USD, and only on USD float, which is short gold. Borrowing USDC against PAXG costs 5.5% on Base Morpho while the prime USDC vaults pay 5.1%, so Tier 0-1 carry on borrowed stables is negative. The net contribution of this stream to a gold-denominated bonus is about 0-1%/yr.

## Gold perps, basis and options

Gold perp funding is a real, repeatable yield, but only for USD-denominated, delta-neutral capital. It does not produce yield in grams, so it cannot fund the current tiers on liability-matching gold.

**What the data shows (all computed from primary APIs, 2026-11-21):**
- Hyperliquid PAXG perp paid shorts 7.7%/yr over 18 months; 12% of hours were negative, the worst 30 days was -1.0% annualised and the worst 90 days +3.1%.
- xyz:GOLD (HIP-3, spot XAU oracle) paid 8.35%/yr since Dec 2025, with a 5.5% design baseline; worst 30 days +0.3%, worst 90 days +4.1%.
- Binance PAXGUSDT paid 4.9% over 19 months, XAUTUSDT 5.2%, and OKX XAU 9.6% over 3 months.
- Funding never went persistently negative through the 36% drawdown. On xyz:GOLD it roughly doubles in rallies (11% in up months vs 6.5% in down months).
- CME gold contango is 4.6-5.2%/yr against SOFR at 3.91%. So most of the funding is the dollar rate; the true excess for taking venue risk is about 1-3%/yr.

**The three trades:**
- **(b) Cash-and-carry** (hold gold token, short the perp) is the usable one. It nets about 5% (range 3-7.5%) on total capital at 3x margin, in USD. Capacity is roughly $10-25M on xyz:GOLD ($255M open interest) and $2-4M on the Hyperliquid PAXG perp. The gold token cannot be margin on Hyperliquid; it can on Derive (tiny cap) and on some CEXs.
- **(a) Synthetic long** (USD yield plus long perp) is negative carry. My backtest shows -3.0% to -6.4%/yr against simply holding PAXG. Use it only as a short-term hedge.
- **(c) CME vs on-chain perp** earns only 0.5-3.3%/yr on notional. It is the same trade as (b) with a futures broker instead of token custody; not worth the setup now.

**Options:** on-chain gold options exist but are small. Derive XAUT options (live since July 2026) have about $9.7M open interest and 27-29% implied vol. Covered-call premiums look like 6-19%/yr, but my model backtest in gold terms returned -7% to +1.7%/yr. It is short volatility and loses grams in rallies.

**What this means for the product:**
- With no reserve (scenario A), perps fund nothing.
- With a partner reserve (scenario B), the clean design is to short perps only against gold held above 100% backing. That funds about 5% × (excess reserve ÷ liabilities) per year. A 30% excess gives about 1.5%/yr of liabilities, which supports headline bonuses of roughly 0.4% / 0.8% / 1.7% on the 6/12/24-month tiers. A 100% excess supports about 1.5% / 2.8% / 5.7%.
- Collateralising user gold to run the trade (scenario D) nets 0-1%/yr in gold terms, and a 50% LTV Morpho loan hits its 77% liquidation threshold at -35%. The actual drawdown was -37%.

**Tail risks are concrete:**
- The PAXG perp wicked -23.6% on 2025-10-10 and +14.4% on 2025-10-16 with gold itself roughly flat.
- xyz:GOLD wicked -4% on two weekends in 2026.
- The same HIP-3 deployer's SK Hynix market had $57-80M of oracle-driven liquidations in July 2026.
- Ostium lost $23.75M and Drift about $280M to key compromises in 2026.

Base-native gold perps are too small to matter (Veranta, the renamed Avantis, has $1.1M of gold open interest).

## Real-economy gold: leasing, miners, partners

Real-economy gold yield exists but is thin, and it is nowhere near the 8.5 / 12.5 / 16.5% gold-terms return the current tiers need.

Wholesale gold is cheap to borrow. COMEX contango is 4.6-5.2%/yr against SOFR at 3.91%, so the implied bank-grade lease rate is about zero; the 3-5% spikes of Jan-Feb 2025 lasted weeks. Leasing to jewellers and refiners pays 2-4% in gold: Monetary Metals averaged 3.91% in 2026 on about 192k oz, GLDY passes through 3.5%, SafeGold pays 2% secured / 4% unsecured, MG999 nets about 1%. Capacity is tens of millions of dollars and the gold leaves allocated custody. Each 1%/yr earned on outstanding grams funds only about 0.33 / 0.58 / 1.08% of bonus on the 6 / 12 / 24-month tiers, so a 3% lease on the whole book funds roughly 1.0 / 1.75 / 3.25%.

Supply-side discounts barely exist on clean metal. Industrial dore pays 99.8% of LBMA less $0.80/oz, with 90% advanced in a day. Central banks buy artisanal gold 0.5-3% below spot, which only covers costs; Ghana's state buyer pays full spot and its programme ran at an 11.7-16% cost. Kilobars trade within about $0.5-2.5/oz of London. Discounts of 5%+ appear only at the mine gate in informal chains, where the fake bars, smuggling (about 435 t/yr out of Africa), seizures and AML exposure sit.

Miner financing: bank prepays to real producers cost about 4-6% in gold terms (B2Gold ~3.8%, i-80 ~5.7%, my calculations). Junior producers and developers pay SOFR+5.5-7.75% plus offtake or streams, roughly 5-8% gold-terms. Only weak credits pay 12-22%: Akobo's 19% gold bond was restructured and now carries 22%. With AISC at $1,930 against $3,530 spot and the producer hedge book at a multi-year low, good miners do not want fixed-ounce capital, so those who do are adverse selection. Deliveries also start 12-21 months after funding, while the product delivers from day 30.

For a partner of unknown standing, the only structure to rely on is gold-in-first. The partner consigns allocated good-delivery gold (or PAXG/XAUT) at deposit-day spot and is paid from a T-bill escrow as grams vest. The platform takes no partner credit or price risk and keeps the escrow yield, worth about 1.2 / 2.1 / 3.9% of principal per tier, plus perhaps 0.3-1% of price discount. A hedged partner would rationally charge that same carry, so this is a marketing subsidy.

Partner-funded bonus ceiling: about 1.5-2% (6m), 2.5-3% (12m), 4-5% (24m). Assume nothing above that until the partner shows audited accounts, unencumbered bar-listed inventory in a recognised vault, LBMA chain of custody and collateral for any exposure.

### Open questions

- Does the partner hedge? An unhedged producer may accept selling at flat spot for deferred payment; a hedged trader or refiner will price at the forward curve and the structure loses its carry.
- Where is the partner's metal physically and in what form (Good Delivery bars in London or Zurich, kilobars in Dubai, dore, or unrefined)? This decides whether it can ever back PAXG/XAUT or needs a separate token and custody chain.
- Would the partner post collateral or accept consignment (deliver first)? Its answer is the cheapest test of its standing.
- What is the partner's own funding cost and all-in production or acquisition cost? These set the most it can rationally give: avoided funding cost minus 4-5% contango if prepaid, or waived carry if paid on delivery.
- Which jurisdiction licenses a platform to lease customer-backing gold or to hold a T-bill escrow against gram liabilities (Switzerland, UAE and Singapore treat these differently)? Not researched here.
- What do lessees actually pay on the lease platforms (the gross rate before the platform's spread), and what security do investors hold on default? Issuer pages were bot-protected and could not be read directly.
- Is there a current published London gold lease or forward-rate benchmark for 2026? None was found; the near-zero implied rate rests on COMEX settles and SOFR.
- What were lender recoveries in the 2024-2025 junior insolvencies (Elevation, Victoria, Ascot)? Needed to calibrate the simulator's 60% recovery assumption for secured miner paper.

## Comparable products and failures

Honest gold-denominated yield tops out around 4% a year, and most of the market sits between 0% and 3%. The current tiers (2.5% / 7% / 18.8%, needing 8.5-16.5% a year in gold) are outside that range.

Distribution, from live or 2026 sources: exchange lending of PAXG/XAUT pays 0.07-0.65% (Binance, OKX); a Turkish bank time gold deposit pays 0.01%; Kinesis fee-share works out to about 0.03%; institutional lease is 1-2%. India's sovereign schemes paid 2.25-2.5% in rupees and were both closed in 2025 as too costly. Tokenised lending wrappers pay 2.3% (thGOLD), 1.5-3% (XAUE) and 3.5% (GLDY). Jeweller leasing pays up to 4% in grams (SafeGold) and 3.96% weighted (Monetary Metals, 192,000 oz). The only honest rates above that are junior-miner credit: Monetary Metals gold bonds at 13% and 19%, the latter restructured in 2025 with an interest holiday.

Higher rates are promotional, opaque or fraudulent. Bybit's 11-23.5% applies to the first 0.1 XAUT (about $350). YouHodler (11%) and CoinDepo (14.5-21.5%) disclose no source; Celsius paid 4.5% on PAXG before failing. The failure record: PIM Gold (bonus gold, 30% markup, 3.38 t owed against a few hundred kg found), IMA (30-36%, fake bars), Goodwin (18% on an 11+1 plan), Jieworui (13.4-18bn yuan frozen, not fully hedged). Even secured leasing has a tail: part of a Monetary Metals lease was seized in Turkey in January 2026.

The closest retail analogue is the Indian jeweller instalment scheme. Tanishq's 10-instalment plan with up to 75% of one instalment as discount is a 7.5% bonus, an implied 12-17% a year; a classic 11+1 is 16-19%. It is paid in jewellery, funded by jewellery margin (making charges 8-25%), and capped by law at 12.5% and 365 days. Titan took Rs 2,116 crore through it in FY26. So a 7% twelve-month bonus is familiar to users, but only where a retail margin pays for it.

The tells that separate honest from Ponzi-like: the rate is at or below what a named borrower pays; liabilities are matched in metal with audited inventory; yield is variable ("up to"), not a guaranteed bonus; no markup or commission tree funds the bonus; redemptions are never gated.

For the product: gold-native yield of 2.5-4% on the float supports roughly 0.7-1.2% / 1.4-2.2% / 2.8-4.6% bonus on the 6/12/24-month tiers (scaled from the brief's own required-return mapping). Anything more must come from USD-yield overlays (other streams), a partner's retail margin, or a marketing budget on capped balances. India's bond experience (Rs 72,275 crore raised, liability about Rs 1.5 lakh crore) and Jieworui both show that gram liabilities must be held in grams.

## Legal structure (research, not advice)

Core finding: five regulators use almost the same test for "backed" assets. The US Model State Commodity Code says "free and clear of all liens and encumbrances". MiCA says "not encumbered nor pledged". VARA says "not rehypothecated, or subject to any pledges". FINMA says "held in readiness for the customer at all times". The SEC/CFTC March 2026 release says "not lent, pledged, or rehypothecated for any reason". Gold posted as collateral, lent or LP'd therefore cannot be counted as allocated or fully backed in any of these regimes. Scenario D on backing gold turns the product into a lending or investment product.

The funding source changes the classification:
- **Supplier or partner discount:** reads as a sale of goods at a better price. Supporting points are Belmont Reid (9th Cir. 1986), FINMA's purchase-prepayment exception, and Singapore's debenture test, which only bites on a predetermined buy-back.
- **Operator treasury yield:** reads as returns from the operator's managerial efforts on pooled customer money. That points to an investment contract in the US, a deposit or collective scheme in Switzerland, and lending or management licences in Dubai.
- **Guaranteed versus variable:** a guaranteed rate pushes toward deposit or debt; a pool-linked variable rate pushes toward a fund.

By jurisdiction:
- **Dubai (VARA):** the only regime surveyed with a rulebook that permits client-facing lending and borrowing with fixed or variable interest, a gold-token (ARVA) issuance regime, and a DMCC tokenised-commodity pathway. The first such ARVA launched 7 September 2026 and was open to retail.
- **Switzerland:** works only for the fully backed, no-yield version. A contractual claim on gold is "probably" a deposit, and the FinTech licence forbids both paying interest and investing.
- **Singapore:** hostile. If the gold token is a payment token, retail lending, staking and incentives are barred. A Singapore entity serving only overseas customers will "generally not" be licensed.
- **EU:** closed. Unauthorised gold tokens cannot be offered, and Article 40's interest ban expressly reaches consenting third-party offerors and time-linked discounts.
- **US:** closed. State commodity codes prohibit prepaid investment contracts without delivery within 28 days.

Recommended design:
1. Buy and lock 100% of principal grams, unencumbered, at purchase.
2. Frame the contractual bonus as a point-of-sale price discount funded by a partner or supplier and by the operator's own margin.
3. Run treasury strategies only with operator or first-loss partner capital in a ring-fenced vehicle.
4. Offer any self-generated yield as a separate, opt-in, variable "earn" tier under a Dubai lending licence.
5. Publish a solvency ratio split into unencumbered gold, encumbered gold net of debt, and non-gold assets.

- **[high]** MiCA Art. 40 bars issuers of asset-referenced tokens, and crypto-asset service providers providing services related to them, from granting interest. 'Interest' includes any benefit related to the length of time a holder holds the token, including 'net compensation or discounts, with an effect equivalent to that of interest', received 'directly from the issuer or from third parties' or 'from the remuneration or pricing of other products'. Source: https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-and-data/interactive-single-rulebook/mica/article-40-prohibition-granting-interest (Reg. (EU) 2023/1114, OJ 9 June 2023; fetched 2026-11-21).

Other persons may offer it with the issuer's written consent, and 'Those persons shall comply with Articles 27, 29 and 40', so the interest prohibition reaches a third-party distributor.
